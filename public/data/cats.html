Cats

A small reference page about domestic cats (Felis catus).

Quick facts

  • Average lifespan: 12–18 years (indoor cats often reach 20+).
  • Typical adult weight: 3.5–5.5 kg (7–12 lb).
  • Normal body temperature: 38.0–39.2 °C (100.4–102.5 °F).
  • Resting heart rate: 140–220 bpm.
  • Gestation period: 63–67 days, average litter 3–5 kittens.
  • Top sprint speed: ~48 km/h (30 mph).
  • Hearing range: 55 Hz – 79 kHz — much wider than humans.
  • Whiskers (vibrissae) are roughly as wide as the cat's body and help gauge gaps.

Diet

Cats are obligate carnivores. They require dietary taurine, arginine, and arachidonic acid — all found in animal tissue. Unlike dogs, cats cannot synthesise enough of these nutrients on their own, so vegetarian diets are generally unsafe for them.

Behaviour notes

  • Purring occurs at 25–150 Hz and may aid tissue healing.
  • Kneading ("making biscuits") is a comfort behaviour carried over from nursing.
  • A slow blink from a cat is often interpreted as a sign of trust.
  • Cats spend 12–16 hours per day sleeping.

Coat and eye variation

Coat colour is driven by a handful of genes controlling pigment type (eumelanin vs. phaeomelanin), pattern (tabby, pointed, solid), and white spotting. Eye colour ranges from copper through gold, green, and blue; complete heterochromia — two differently coloured eyes — occurs most often in cats with a dominant white gene and is harmless.

Tabby patterns

The tabby coat is the ancestral pattern and comes in four main variants: mackerel (vertical stripes), classic (swirls or "blotched"), spotted, and ticked (agouti bands without clear markings). Almost all cats carry the tabby gene — even solid-coloured cats often show faint "ghost" tabby markings under direct sunlight.
